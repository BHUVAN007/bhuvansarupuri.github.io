Why I built it

We had plenty of evidence. We could not see how it fit together.

01

No map of the product

The docs describe screens, the API describes endpoints, and research describes pains. Nothing lined up per task, so every project started by rediscovering how a module works.

02

Data in too many places

Research sessions, satisfaction comments, support tickets, usage data and surveys each held part of the picture. Combining them for one question took days.

03

Symptoms, not causes

Problems were raised and fixed one at a time. Nobody could see that most of them grow from the same few design choices, or which fix would unlock the others.

The root-cause map

Untangling 574 problems into 12 causes, and how they feed each other.

Every problem from the research was coded to an underlying cause. The causes stack in four layers, from what the product covers to what people feel in their daily work. The arrows are the dependencies: one cause making the next one worse. Click any cause to untangle it.

🔒Details are blurred on purpose. The cause names, findings and counts are confidential TOPdesk research. The structure, the dependencies between causes and the interactions are shown as built.

Each box is a cause, with the number of problems linked to it and the modules it shows up in. Solid arrows are backed by specific problems in the research; dashed arrows are inferred. Counts show how much of the research points to a cause, not how many customers are affected.

    How it is used today

    Four jobs the Atlas does for the team.

    Map processes

    Understand a module before touching it

    Roles, tasks, card fields and lifecycle in one read. Designers and researchers use it to get up to speed on an unfamiliar module, and to see what a redesign has to keep.

    See data in one place

    Answer "how bad is it?" without seven tools

    For any task, the research, satisfaction comments, support topics, usage and release status sit side by side, with contradictions between sources kept visible.

    Find root causes

    Move from symptoms to causes

    The map shows which cause sits upstream of which, so the team can pick fixes that unlock others instead of chasing each complaint on its own.

    Spot automation

    Find what is worth automating

    Steps where customers already build their own automation point to behaviour the product could offer natively, and to where automation needs better logs and warnings.
