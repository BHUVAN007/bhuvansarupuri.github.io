Rethinking Services Around Customer Outcomes

Cross-domain discovery, workflow transformation and future-state service design.

I led research and service innovation work spanning several product areas and departments, to understand how existing workflows operate and where they could improve. The work started from how things run today, and ended with future-state service blueprints that informed the product roadmap.

Understanding the current state

Mapping how work actually crosses teams and systems.

The work began by mapping current-state processes: how different teams and systems interact, where work is handed over, and where workflow challenges and dependencies sit. Many of the problems only become visible when a process is followed across product and organisational boundaries, rather than within one product area.

Rethinking from outcomes

Starting from what users want to achieve, not from each existing step.

I then facilitated cross-functional workshops to rethink these workflows from the perspective of what users ultimately want to achieve. Rather than starting with how AI or another technology could automate each existing step, we examined the underlying customer goals, challenged assumptions about current processes, and explored how services could be redesigned around those outcomes.

Two complementary moves

Improving the workflows people have today and rethinking them around outcomes are not in competition. Making an existing step faster still matters; this work added the question of whether the step should exist in that form at all.

Future-state service design

Blueprints that connect customer needs to product capabilities.

I translated the workshops into future-state service blueprints. Each one connects three layers that are usually discussed separately:

Customer
Needs and goals
What the user is trying to get done, end to end.
Organisation
Internal processes
The teams, hand-offs and systems behind the service.
Product
Potential capabilities
What the product could do to support the outcome.
Outcome

Five features on the current product roadmap.

The research and service design work informed five features included in the current product roadmap. Being on the roadmap is not the same as being shipped, and this page does not claim that they have.

More broadly, the project shows how research can help teams both improve and rethink end-to-end experiences, and find opportunities that sit across product and organisational boundaries.
